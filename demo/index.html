    About this demo

    A device that may detonate has to be moved from A to the disposal site B. The best route is the one that minimises the damage an accidental detonation along the way would cause to the buildings around it, weighted by how critical they are.

    The pipeline has three layers, as in the original project: a cheap empirical blast law with building masking (Kinney–Graham, a Kingery–Bulmash-type scaled-distance law); a Gaussian process that learns only the residual between a simulator and that law, with an acquisition rule that picks the few positions worth simulating for the current route; and a Delta-Stepping shortest-path search on the corrected danger map.

    The simulator here is a toy oracle: a linear 2D acoustic wave solver (no shocks), calibrated so that in open space it reproduces the blast law exactly. It differs from the law only through reflections, diffraction and shadowing, which is what the Gaussian process has to learn.

    Toy reimplementation for illustration only: synthetic scene, simple 2D acoustic solver; no code, data or results from the original CEA-DAM project.
