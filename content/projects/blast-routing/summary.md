An explosive-ordnance team must move a device that may detonate from A to B through a built-up area. The goal is the route that minimizes expected damage to the surrounding buildings, weighted by their criticality. One run of the reference blast code, ARMEN, takes 10 minutes in 2D and 2 hours on 48 cores in 3D, so an exhaustive 50 × 50 map would cost about 5,000 hours. We pair a Kingery–Bulmash physical prior with a Gaussian process that learns only its residual, let an acquisition loop choose the few positions worth simulating, then search the corrected map. In 2D, 19 simulations out of 10,000 candidate positions (15 initial, 4 chosen by the loop) cut the relative uncertainty on the route corridor from 14.5 % to 4.0 %. In 3D, the pipeline runs end to end on the partner's supercomputer and returns a route over 2,500 terrain nodes in under 10 s once the map is built.
