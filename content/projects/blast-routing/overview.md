- **The brief.** The CEA-DAM asked how to turn high-fidelity blast simulations into a decision someone can act on. An explosive-ordnance team must move a device that may detonate from A to B through a built-up area; the team had to find the route that minimizes expected damage to the surrounding buildings, weighted by their criticality.
- **Cost.** One run of the reference blast code, ARMEN, takes 10 minutes in 2D and 2 hours on 48 cores in 3D, so an exhaustive 50 × 50 map would cost about 5,000 hours.
- **Method.** A Kingery–Bulmash physical prior gives a danger map in seconds; a Gaussian process learns only its residual, from the few positions an acquisition loop chooses to simulate; a graph search then extracts the route from the corrected map.
- **Outcome.** In 2D, 19 simulations out of 10,000 candidate positions (15 initial, 4 chosen by the loop) cut the relative uncertainty on the route corridor from 14.5 % to 4.0 %. In 3D, the pipeline runs end to end on the partner's supercomputer and returns a route over 2,500 terrain nodes in under 10 s once the map is built.
