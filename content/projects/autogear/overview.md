- **The idea.** A box that clips onto any bike and shifts its gears by itself, so that the rider keeps a steady, comfortable effort at traffic lights, on hills and in the wind: the comfort of an automatic gearbox, at a fraction of the price of an e-bike. The only automatic derailleur on the market costs about €1,500; the riders we surveyed would pay €100–120.
- **From startup to R&D.** It began in CentraleSupélec's entrepreneurship track, with a market study, user tests and a business plan. The hard part turned out to be technical: deciding *when* to shift, from cheap sensors, on a small board. Step by step, the project became R&D in embedded machine learning.
- **The constraint that shaped everything.** No torque sensor: it would make the box more expensive and harder to fit on any bike. The system only sees the pedalling cadence and an inertial unit (acceleration, rotation, slope), so the decision has to be learned from partial signals.
- **What exists.** A fourth hardware version, plug and play: our own cadence sensor, an embedded electronics box built around an Arduino, 3D-printed parts and a simple mount. About sixty riders tried the proof of concept and the prototype at sports events (comfort 7.8/10, relevance 7.1/10).
