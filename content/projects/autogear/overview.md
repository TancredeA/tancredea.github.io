- **The product.** The Shifter Pro: a box that clips onto any bike and shifts its gears by itself, for urban and occasional riders: the comfort of an automatic gearbox, at a fraction of the price of an e-bike. The only automatic derailleur on the market costs about €1,500; the riders we surveyed would pay €100–120.
- **The constraint that shaped everything.** No torque sensor: it is one of the most expensive parts of an automatic drivetrain, and leaving it out is the premise of the product. The box sees only the cadence, an inertial unit and a barometer, so the gear has to be chosen from partial signals, on a small board.
- **What the team built.** It began in CentraleSupélec's entrepreneurship track, with a market study, user tests and a business plan. A fourth hardware version, plug and play: our own cadence sensor, an embedded electronics box built around an Arduino, a servo acting on the bike's own derailleur. About sixty riders tried the proof of concept and the prototype at sports events (comfort 7.8/10, relevance 7.1/10).
- **The missing data.** The last prototype was modified to record rides, so that the model would learn from our own shifting. Lack of time and bad weather meant those rides were never recorded, and the prototype is no longer available: the extension below starts from there.
