- **The product.** The Shifter Pro: a box that clips onto any bike and shifts its gears by itself, for urban and occasional riders: the comfort of an automatic gearbox, at a fraction of the price of an e-bike. The only automatic derailleur on the market costs about €1,500; the riders we surveyed would pay €100–120.
- **The constraint that shaped everything.** No torque sensor: it is one of the most expensive parts of an automatic drivetrain, and leaving it out is the premise of the product. The box sees only the cadence, an inertial unit and a barometer, so the gear has to be chosen from partial signals, on a small board.
- **What we built.** The whole chain, plug and play: our own cadence sensor, an embedded electronics box built around an Arduino, a servo acting on the bike's own derailleur, and the model that chooses the gear, exported to C for the board. Around it, a market study, user tests and a business plan, in CentraleSupélec's entrepreneurship track.
- **What riders said.** About sixty riders tried an early prototype that already shifted by itself, at sports events: comfort 7.8/10, relevance 7.1/10.
