- **Reconstructed, not recorded.** The data come from six ordinary riders on head units, not from the team riding the prototype: gears are inferred (84 % correct where they could be checked), the sensors differ from the box's, and 18.9 h is little.
- **Simulated, not ridden.** The closed loop is replayed with speed held fixed, and there has been no field test: the Arduino integration sketch has never run on the bike. The results suggest the learned policy chooses gears better than a decision table and make it reasonable to expect it to be at least as good; only a test on the bike can confirm it.
- **Personal by nature.** Trained on five riders and tested on the sixth, the learned policy falls to 72.5 %, below the torque-aware table (75.2 %): the product has to learn each rider, starting from a decision table or the linear regression until about half an hour of riding is recorded.
- **It follows, it does not anticipate.** Its shifts come about 1.7 s after the rider's; the persistence of the controller sets that delay, not the horizon. Faster downshifts (1 s instead of 3 s) reach 88.0 %, the natural next setting to test.
