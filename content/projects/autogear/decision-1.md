*Problem.* A rider shifts by feel, and the most telling signal is the torque on the pedals. A torque sensor is also one of the most expensive parts of an automatic drivetrain, and hard to fit on just any bike.

*Choice.* Leave it out, and treat shifting as learning from demonstration under partial observation: the model sees only what the box measures and learns the gear choices of a reference rider. History stands in for effort: means and trends of speed and slope over the last 3 to 15 s.

*Why.* The premise holds up: on the reconstructed rides, giving the torque to a decision table adds only +0.3 points of agreement with the rider. What a table lacks is the rider's habits, and a model can learn them from the recent past of signals the box already has.
