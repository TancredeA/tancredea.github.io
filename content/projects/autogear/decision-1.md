*Problem.* A rider shifts by feel: the most telling signal is the torque on the pedals. A torque sensor would make the box expensive and hard to fit on just any bike.

*Choice.* Leave it out, and treat shifting as learning from demonstration under partial observation: the model sees only cadence and inertial data, and learns the shifts of an experienced cyclist.

*Why.* At the same cadence and acceleration, a relaxed rider and a straining one call for different decisions; a single sample cannot tell them apart, but a few seconds of history can, since a straining rider's cadence drops soon after. So every input carries its recent past.
