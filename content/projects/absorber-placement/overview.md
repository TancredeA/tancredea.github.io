- **The brief.** Absorbing material is costly, so a concert hall is rarely treated wall to wall. Given a fixed quantity of porous material, the team had to find where to put it on the walls, on which wall shape and how much, so as to minimize the acoustic energy in the hall.
- **Model.** A 44 m × 28 m slice of a shoebox hall modeled on the Concertgebouw, driven by a music source on the stage. Sound obeys the Helmholtz equation; the material enters through a complex Robin condition weighted by its density χ on the wall.
- **Method.** The acoustic energy is minimized by projected gradient descent under a mass constraint: each iteration solves the direct problem for the pressure p and an adjoint problem for q, which together give the gradient. The loop was run over several frequencies.
- **Outcome.** The team fixed the design one parameter at a time: mineral wool, 50 % coverage, then a level-2 pre-fractal wall (level 3 is hard to build for a marginal gain). On a flat wall, mineral wool lowers resonance peaks by a median of two orders of magnitude relative to a solid panel.
