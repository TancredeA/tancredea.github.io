A concert hall is rarely treated wall to wall: absorbing material is costly. Given a fixed quantity, where should it go, on which wall shape, and how much? The hall is modeled by a Helmholtz problem in which the material enters through a complex Robin condition weighted by its density χ on the wall, and the acoustic energy is minimized by projected gradient descent under a mass constraint. My part is the model's foundations: a physically grounded music source, the proof that the problem is well posed with an absorbing wall, and the adjoint formula that gives the gradient at every wall point for two PDE solves. The team retained mineral wool on half of a level-2 pre-fractal wall; on a flat wall, mineral wool lowers resonance peaks by a median of two orders of magnitude relative to a solid panel.
