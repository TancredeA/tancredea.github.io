- **No breakthrough in compression or scalability.** Tensorised layers have compressed networks since 2015 (Novikov et al.) and 2020 (Gao et al.); the paper compares no parameter counts with them, and its own training time grows exponentially with the number of tensors.
- **What is new is a combination.** Tensor (MPO) layers sit inside a hybrid deep network, among dense layers, and are trained by two-site variational sweeps borrowed from DMRG instead of back-propagation. Each half existed before: tensor layers in ordinary networks were trained by back-propagation, DMRG-style sweeps were used only for standalone tensor-network models.
- **Interpretability while the network trains.** That combination makes the entanglement spectrum of the weights readable at every step of training. Entanglement had been used to read tensor-network models before (Levine et al., 2017; Liu et al., 2018; Martyn et al., 2020), but never inside a neural network as it trains.
- **It mirrors the data.** Well-separated Gaussian blobs give an entropy near zero; three intertwined spirals keep it around 0.7–0.8. A single number read off the network already says something about the geometry of what it learned.
